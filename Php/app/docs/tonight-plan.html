Tonight — the plan

Partial I is tomorrow at 12:30, and she has an exam in another subject as well. So tonight is split, and this is built around the slice PHP actually gets — not around covering everything.

Give PHP 45 minutes. That is the plan. It covers 38 of the 50 marks. There is a 90-minute version below if PHP turns out to be the weaker of the two, and a 10-minute pass in the morning either way.

Splitting the evening between two subjects

  • Do the subject she is weaker at first, while she is fresh — not the one she enjoys.
  • One block each, with a real break between them. Two subjects interleaved is how both get half-learned.
  • Finish on the subject sat first tomorrow. Whatever is revised last sits nearest the surface.
  • Sleep beats the last hour. Going to bed at a sane time is worth more marks than a fourth pass over either subject, and she has two papers to stay sharp for.
  • If the other subject is heavy memorisation and PHP is not, give PHP the 45, not the 90. PHP's 38 marks come from a skeleton and twelve facts — it rewards a short sharp block far better than a long one.

90 minutes — only if PHP is the weaker subject

0:00 – 0:35 · Section 4, the skeleton (20 marks)

Not reading. Writing, from blank, on paper.

  1. (5 min) Read the skeleton on the revision page once.
  2. (15 min) Write it out from memory — pseudocode, flowchart, PHP — for this prompt: five student names and five marks, print each one's name, mark and PASS/FAIL (pass is 60), and say who is highest and lowest.
  3. (10 min) Check it against the answer. Mark it honestly.
  4. (5 min) Write out only the parts she got wrong, again.

If she can produce the skeleton from blank, Section 4 is safe. If she cannot, nothing else tonight matters as much — stay here.

0:35 – 0:50 · Section 1 drill (10 marks)

Flashcards or straight questioning, out loud, fast. Twelve facts:

  1. PHP runs on the server, before the HTML reaches the browser.
  2. PHP = Hypertext Preprocessor. Interpreted, no compilation.
  3. Valid name: $ then a letter or underscore. $2php ✗ $bad-punc ✗ abc ✗
  4. Case matters. $Name ≠ $name.
  5. The dot joins. The plus adds. 10 . 5 → 105, 10 + 5 → 15.
  6. Division always makes a float. 56 / 12 → 4.666…
  7. (int) 9.9 → 9. It chops, it does not round.
  8. == converts then compares. === compares value and type.
  9. do…while is the one-trip loop. while is zero-trip.
  10. break ends the loop. continue ends only this pass.
  11. Comments: //, #, /* */. Not <!-- --> — that is HTML.
  12. "A" . FALSE . "B" → AB. "X" . TRUE . "Y" → X1Y.

0:50 – 1:00 · Section 2 drill (8 marks)

Same twelve facts, asked as true/false. He writes TF from the same material as MCQ — if she has the twelve, she has both sections. 18 marks for 25 minutes of talking.

1:00 – 1:25 · Section 3, tracing (12 marks)

Question 3 on the questions page. She writes the output. You check it.

Then one rule, which is the whole section: trace with a table, never in your head. A column per variable, a row per pass. Every mark lost in Section 3 is lost by someone who tried to hold it in their head.

1:25 – 1:30 · The five sentences

Read these out. Nothing else.

  • Write the $ on every variable.
  • Join with the dot. The plus will quietly turn her words into numbers.
  • In an if, == asks a question. A single = puts a value in and is always true.
  • Every flowchart needs a START and a STOP, and nothing left hanging.
  • Heading rows and <table> go outside the loop. Only <tr> goes inside.

Tomorrow morning — 10 minutes, no more

  1. Read the twelve facts once. (3 min)
  2. Write the Section 4 PHP skeleton from blank, once, on paper. (5 min)
  3. Read the five sentences. (2 min)

Then stop. Nothing new on the morning of an exam — it displaces what is already in there.

In the hall

Order to answer in: 1 → 2 → 4 → 3.

Do Section 4 third, not last. It is 20 marks and needs the most time; if the clock beats her it must not be the thing left unwritten. Tracing is the one to leave till the end, because a half-traced answer can still score.

Within Section 4: pseudocode first, then flowchart, then code. The flowchart is just the pseudocode drawn, and the code is just the pseudocode typed. Doing them in that order means thinking once, writing three times.

  • 12 minutes on Sections 1 and 2.
  • 25 minutes on Section 4.
  • 17 minutes on Section 3.
  • 6 minutes to check: every $ present, every flowchart has START and STOP, every brace closed.

If she blanks on Section 4, write the pseudocode anyway. It is 5 of the 20 marks and it needs no PHP at all.
